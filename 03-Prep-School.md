# Track 1

## Species

A group of animals or plants that are very similar and can have babies together.

## Urban

Related to a city or town.

## Loan

Money that you borrow and must pay back later, usually with interest.

## Several

More than two but not many; a few.

## Set off

To start a journey or to cause something to happen.

## Board

It can also mean to get on a vehicle, like a train or plane.

## Get to

To reach or arrive at a place; it can also mean to have the opportunity to do something.

## Reach

To arrive at a place, especially after spending a long time or a lot of effort travelling.

## Land

It can also mean to arrive on the ground after flying or to come down from the air.

## Take off

To leave the ground and begin to fly, like an airplane.

## Bargain

An agreement between people on the price of something; it can also mean a good deal or a sale.

## Give something away

To give something for free, without expecting anything in return.

## Receipt

A written piece of paper that shows you have paid for something or received goods.

## Afford

To have enough money to buy something.

## Borrow

To take something from someone with the promise to give it back later.

## Lend

To give something to someone for a short time, expecting them to return it later.

## Owe

To need to pay or give something to someone because they have lent money to you.

## Discount

A reduction in the usual price.

## Magnificent

Very beautiful, impressive, or grand; something that is very great or wonderful.

## Ordinary

Common; not special or different; usual.

## Tiny

Very small; much smaller than usual.

## Have a shave

To remove hair from your face, usually with a razor or shaver.

## Pollute

To make something dirty or harmful, especially the air, water, or land, by adding waste or chemicals.

## Commuter

A person who travels regularly between home and work or school, usually by public transportation or car.

## Outskirts

The areas on the edge of a city or town, away from the center.

## Congestion

A situation where there is too much traffic or too many people in a place, causing difficulty in movement or flow.

## Contain

To have something inside or include something as a part. 

**Example:** How much liquid do you think this bottle contains?

## Empty

Not containing any things or people. 

**Example:** Shall I take the empty bottles for recycling?

## Bank clerk

An employee of a bank who deals with customers, receives and pays out money, etc.

## Employee

Someone who is paid to work for someone else.

## Plumber

A person whose job is to supply and connect or repair water pipes, baths, toilets, etc.

## Accountant

A person who keeps financial records, prepares reports, and helps manage money for individuals or businesses.

## Fairly

More than a little but not very much.

## Annoy

To make someone angry. 

**Example:** Tina really annoyed me in the meeting this morning.

## Confuse

To make someone unable to think clearly or understand something. 

**Example:** The complicated instructions confused him, and he didn’t know what to do.

## Embarrassed

Feeling shy, uncomfortable, or ashamed, often because of something awkward or silly.

## Excited

Eager about something.

## Lack of

Not having enough of something, or having none at all. 

**Example:** The plants died due to a lack of water.

## A rash

A group of red spots on the skin that can be itchy or painful, usually caused by an allergy, illness, or reaction. 

**Example:** He developed a rash on his arm after touching the plant.

## Get a prescription

To receive a written order from a doctor for medicine or treatment.

## The flu

A common illness caused by a virus, with symptoms like fever, cough, sore throat, and body aches.

## Fee

A payment made for a service or for permission to do something.

## Essential

Something that is very important or necessary.

## Prevent

To stop something from happening or someone from doing something.

## Illustrate

To explain or show something clearly, often with pictures or examples.

## Curious

Wanting to know or learn more about something. 

**Example:** The child was curious about how the clock works.

## Feature

An important part, quality, or characteristic of something.

## Monument

A building, statue, or structure made to honor a person or event. 

**Example:** The Taj Mahal is a famous monument in India.

## Experiment

A test done to learn something new or to see if an idea is correct.

## Brave

Showing courage and not being afraid in difficult or dangerous situations. 

**Example:** The firefighter was brave and rescued the children from the fire.

## Inherit

To receive something from someone after they pass away, usually property or money.

## Distinguish

To see or show the difference between two or more things. 

**Example:** It's hard to distinguish between the real and fake paintings.

## Debt

Money that you owe to someone and need to pay back.

## Clever

Quick to understand or learn; showing intelligence or creativity. 

**Example:** She came up with a clever idea to solve the problem.

## Phenomenon

An event or situation that can be observed, especially one that is unusual or difficult to explain.

## Observed

Noticed or seen something, usually with attention or careful thought.

## Repetitive

Describing something that is repeated many times, often in a way that feels monotonous or boring.

## Boredom

The feeling of being tired or uninterested because something is not exciting or engaging.

## Cognitive

Relating to mental processes such as thinking, learning, memory, and understanding.

## Manufacture

To make something in large quantities.

## Invest(v)

To put money, time, or effort into something with the hope of gaining a benefit or profit in the future.

## Investment(n)

The act of putting money, time, or effort into something to achieve a future benefit or profit.

## Recession

A period when the economy slows down, leading to less business activity, job losses, and lower spending.

## Violent

Using force to hurt or damage something or someone. 

**Example:** The violent storm broke many trees in the park.

## Current

Happening or existing now. 

**Example:** The current situation is very difficult for many people.

## Competition

A situation where people or groups try to win or be better than others. 

**Example:** The competition between the two teams was very exciting.

## Consultant

A person who gives expert advice in a particular area. 

**Example:** The company hired a consultant to help improve their marketing strategy.

## Ornament

An object used to decorate something.

## Remarkable

Something that is very special or unusual.

## Talent

A natural ability to do something well. 

**Example:** She has a talent for playing the piano.

## Approach

A way of doing or thinking about something. 

**Example:** His approach to solving problems is very creative.
