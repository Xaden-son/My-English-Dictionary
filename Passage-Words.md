# These words come from some prep school passing exams

## Speculate

To guess possible answers to a question when you do not have enough information to be certain.

**Example:** I don't know why she did it - I'm just speculating.

## Leap

To make a large jump or sudden movement, usually from one place to another.

**Example:** He leaped out of his car and ran towards the house.

**Example:** I leaped up to answer the phone.

## Extensive

Covering a large area; having a great range.

**Example:** A school with extensive grounds.(extensive ground)

**Example:** Extensive repairs to the motorway(extensive repairs)

## Whaling

The activity of hunting whales.

## Naval

Belonging to a country's navy, or relating to military ships.

**Example:** a naval officer,naval forces, a naval museum/battle

## Navy

The part of a country's armed forces that is trained to operate at sea.

**Example:** My brother is an officer in the Navy.

## Armaments

Weapons or military equipment.

**Example:** The country has a long history of heavy engineering and armaments production.

## Voyage

A long journey, especially by ship.

**Example:** The Titanic sank on its maiden voyage in 1912.(maiden voyage)

**Example:** He was a young sailor on his first sea voyage.(on a voyage)

## Maiden Voyage

The first journey made by a ship or spacecraft.

## Spacecraft

A vehicle used for travel in space.

## Tale

A story, especially one that might be invented or difficult to believe.

**Example:** He told some fascinating tales about his life in India.

## Diminish

To reduce or be reduced in size or importance.

**Example:** I don't want to diminish her achievements, but she did have a lot of help.

**Example:** These memories will not be diminished by time.

## Fracuture

If something hard, such as a bone, fractures, or is fractured, it breaks or cracks

**Example:** The impact fractured his elbow.

## Rib

A bone that curves round from your back to your chest

**Example:** My son broke a rib when he fell off a ladder.

**Example:** Two of her ribs fractured when she was thrown from her horse.

## Ladder

A piece of equipment used for climbing up and down, that consists of two vertical bars or pieces of rope joined to each other by a set of horizontal steps

**Example:** She was up a ladder, cleaning the window.(up a ladder)

## Weave

To make cloth by repeatedly crossing a single thread through two sets of long threads on a loom (= special frame).

**Example:** This type of wool is woven into fabric which will make jackets.(be woven into)

## Wool 

The soft, thick hair that grows on the bodies of sheep and some other animals.

## Resonate 

Here, “resonate” means to connect with people emotionally or feel meaningful/relevant to them.

**Example:** This tradition resonates with many young people.

## Quirky

Unusual in an attractive and interesting way.

**Example:** He was tall and had a quirky, off-beat sense of humour.

## Offbeat

Unusual and strange and therefore surprising or noticeable.

**Example:** An offbeat sense of humour.

## Vibrant

Energetic, exciting, and full of enthusiasm.

**Example:** a vibrant young performer , a vibrant personality , a vibrant city.

## Precision

The quality of being exact.

**Example:** Great precision is required to align the mirrors accurately.

## Transient

Lasting for only a short time; temporary.

**Example:** A glass of whisky has only a transient warming effect.

**Example:** The city has a large transient population (= many people who are living in it only temporarily).

##  Deceased

Dead.

**Example:** The recently deceased Member of Parliament

## Tribute

Something that you say, write, or give that shows your respect and admiration for someone, especially on a formal occasion.

**Example:** Tributes have been pouring in from all over the world for the famous actor who died yesterday.

## Embrace

To hold someone tightly with both arms to express love, liking, or sympathy, or when greeting or leaving someone.

**Example:** He leaned over to embrace the child.

## Riotous

Very loud and uncontrolled, and full of energy.

**Example:** We went to a riotous party and danced all night.

## Vastly 

Very much.

**Example:** vastly different, vastly improved.

## Essence

The basic or most important idea or quality of something.

**Example:** The essence of his argument was that education should continue throughout life.

## Foster 

To take care of a child, usually for a limited time, without being the child's legal parent.

**Example:** Would you consider fostering a child?(foster a child)

## Expedition

An organized journey for a particular purpose.

**Example:** We're going on a shopping expedition on Saturday.(go on an expedition)

**Example:** Scott died while he was on an expedition to the Antarctic in 1912.(on an expedition)

## Vessel

A large boat or a ship.

**Example:** A warning went out to fishing vessels in the area.(fishing vessel)

## Specimen

Something shown or examined as an example; a typical example.

**Example:** He has a collection of rare insect specimens.

**Example:** Museums will pay large amounts of money for good dinosaur fossil specimens.

## Descend

To go or come down.

**Example:** The path descended steeply into the valley.

## Pavement

A path with a hard surface on one or both sides of a road, that people walk on.

**Example:** Keep to the pavement, Rosie, there's a good girl.

## Shorthand

A system of fast writing that uses lines and simple signs to represent words and phrases.

**Example:** Their conversations were taken down in shorthand by a secretary.(in shorthand)

## Shorthand Typist

Someone who types and does shorthand as the main part of their job.

## Comb

You can shape your hair with this item.

**Example:** His hair looked as though it had never seen a comb and he himself could have done with a bath.

## Rascal

A person, especially a child or a man, who does things that you disapprove of, but who you still like.

**Example:** I caught those little/young rascals dressing up in my clothes.

## Disrupt

To prevent something, especially a system, process, or event, from continuing as usual or as expected.

**Example:** Everyday life in countries such as Sudan, Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia and Zaire is constantly disrupted. 

## Aid

A piece of equipment that helps you to do something

**Example:** teaching aids, such as books and videos

## Warring

See at war.

## Reunion

A social event for a group of people who have not seen each other for a long time.

**Example:** We're having a family reunion next week.

**Example:** The college has an annual reunion for former students.

## Flounder

To experience great difficulties or be completely unable to decide what to do or say next.

**Example:** He lost the next page of his speech and floundered around/about for a few seconds.

## Enrich

To improve the quality of something by adding something else.

**Example:** My life was greatly enriched by knowing her.

## Acquaintance

A person that you have met but do not know well.

**Example:** a business acquaintance

## Precept

A rule for action or behaviour, especially obtained from moral thought.

**Example:** This policy goes against common precepts of decency.

## Decency

Behaviour that is good, moral, and acceptable in society.

**Example:** She didn't even have the decency to apologize.

## Nurture

To take care of, feed, and protect someone or something, especially young children or plants, and help him, her, or it to develop.

**Example:** She wants to stay at home and nurture her children.

## Perception

A belief or opinion, often held by many people and based on how things seem.

**Example:** We have to change the public's perception that money is being wasted.

## Rover

A person who spends their time travelling from place to place.

**Example:**Their uncle was a rover, never settling for long in any one place.

## Registry

A place where official records are kept.

**Example:**  a land/business/electoral registry

## Aspire

To want something very much or hope to achieve something or be successful.

**Example:** Most of his students aspired to a career in business.(aspire to sth)
